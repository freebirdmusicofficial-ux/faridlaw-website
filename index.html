Our Practice Meet the Partners
Two names, one practice — built around the law as it actually intersects with media, technology, and commerce in Pakistan today.

Farid & Farid is a boutique law firm founded by Yahya Farid Khwaja and Mariam Farid Khwaja. The firm was built around the practice areas shaping Pakistan's modern economy — intellectual property, media and entertainment, technology and digital regulation, capital markets, and constitutional law.

We advise individuals, businesses, creators, and organizations navigating complex legal and regulatory questions, with particular depth in matters involving digital platforms, broadcasting and media, emerging technology, corporate affairs, and constitutional law. Our work also extends to digital rights and online safety — including advocacy for stronger legal protections for children online.

The Partners

Yahya Farid Khwaja
Founding Partner

A Pakistani lawyer whose practice spans Intellectual Property, Media & Entertainment Law, Capital Markets, Technology Law, Company Law, and Constitutional matters. Yahya advises individuals, businesses, creators, and organizations on legal and regulatory issues involving intellectual property rights, digital platforms, media, emerging technologies, corporate affairs, and constitutional law.

He has also been actively involved in matters concerning digital rights, online safety, and technology regulation in Pakistan — including advocacy for stronger legal protections for children online and appropriate legislative frameworks addressing social media use, cyberbullying, online harassment, exploitation, and harmful digital content.

Mariam Farid Khwaja
Partner

An Advocate of the High Courts of Pakistan with experience across Intellectual Property Rights, Media & Entertainment Law, Digital Media, Regulatory Affairs, Commercial and Corporate Law, Constitutional Law, Banking, Finance, and Tax matters.

Mariam has worked on complex commercial and regulatory matters alongside leading commercial law practitioners in Pakistan, and served as legal manager of a private limited company during the process of securing Pakistan's first Direct-to-Home (DTH) Pay TV licence awarded by PEMRA — giving her practical exposure to the country's media and broadcasting regulatory landscape. She writes on music copyright protection, digital platforms, regulatory policy, and legal system reform, and brings board-level corporate governance experience to her practice.
